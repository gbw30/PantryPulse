The chosen design, three levels of polish

Same layout and decisions (list + big map, site replaces list, story + chart, routes only for the picked site, summary on top, Run forecast in the bar, county lines + cities). Only the finish changes. Click a row or a marker, then Back, Prev/Next, "Show as table" and Run forecast. Brand colors and IBM Plex throughout; transitions are 200 ms on the app's existing curve and turn instant under reduced motion.

V1 · Refined desk

Today's brand, decluttered: white sidebar, chalk map, hairline rules, teal used only for selection and actions.

Pros

  • Closest to what's built: least rework, lowest risk before the W6 candidate
  • Most legible at 1280×720; plain sans everywhere
  • Consistent with Import, Decisions and Settings screens

Cons

  • The least "premium"; reads as a good tool rather than a crafted one
  • Boxy sidebar edge competes slightly with the map

V2 · Editorial

The premium-gallery move: IBM Plex Serif for the headline, site name and story sentence; warm paper tones; wide-tracked small caps for group labels; fewer lines, more air.

Pros

  • Feels calm and considered, like a briefing, which fits "story first"
  • Same type family (Plex), so the brand stays intact
  • Small change to build: fonts, spacing, a few tokens

Cons

  • The serif must then appear elsewhere too, or Overview looks like a different app
  • Serif at small sizes reads slower; kept to headings and the one sentence only
  • Changes ART-DIRECTION (a recorded decision)

V3 · Floating panels

The map runs edge to edge; the list and the top bar float above it as soft rounded cards (the Apple/Google Maps look).

Pros

  • The most "app-like" and immersive; the map is the whole screen
  • The most familiar to people who use maps apps daily

Cons

  • The card covers part of the map, so markers can hide under it; fitting the view must avoid the card
  • Shadows and overlap cost more build and test work (focus, axe, 390 px)
  • Departs most from the other screens

Recommended: V2's type and tones on V1's structure. The serif for the headline, site name and story sentence gives the premium feel the galleries share, at a small build cost, without the hidden-markers risk of V3. It would need a one-line ART-DIRECTION amendment, which is your call.
