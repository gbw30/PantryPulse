1. Product Overview
1.V4 Authoritative Extensions
1.6 Hackathon-qualifying discovery and product commitment
P0 ELIGIBILITY COMMITMENT — The defining new discovery is the Extended Goal Programming with Poverty Incorporation (EGP-PI) formulation published by Rampazo, Jones, Godoi, Iamamoto and Cantane on September 11, 2026. The hackathon eligibility window is October 23, 2025 through October 23, 2026; cite the first-publication evidence in §20. Chronos-2 and standard OR-Tools are supporting technologies, not the eligibility claim.
CENTRAL APPLICATION — PantryPulse must translate the paper's distinctive absolute-poverty (AP) and relative-poverty (RP) goal structures and attribute-based vulnerability penalties into the allocation model. Actual candidate allocations, solver objectives, API outputs, tests, and judging demo must depend on those constructs. The project is research-derived, not a verbatim reproduction of every research equation.
COMPETITIVE CLAIM — 'Using a September 2026 poverty-incorporating goal-programming discovery to reduce scarcity and compare equitable food allocation against conventional optimization.' If the AP/RP-inspired mechanism is not working, the team must not present this as an implementation of that 2026 discovery.
BOUNDARY — The selected method acts on food-bank allocation decisions; forecasting, inventory reconciliation, FEFO, procurement proposals, and UI provide inputs and supporting workflows, not the qualifying invention.

V4 integrates the full V1 reference specification, adds the latest V3 revisions, and supersedes older priority or automation rules whenever the two conflict. This is a canonical product specification, not permission to execute real purchases.
• PantryPulse predicts where supplies will run short, balances uncertain incoming donations against expected demand, recommends internal transfers or supplier replenishment, and replans when conditions change. It aims to minimize unmet demand, projected spoilage, unfair shortfalls, and operational costs while satisfying hard capacity, expiry, and inventory constraints.
• The product should answer: (1) What is in stock and usable now? (2) What supply and demand do we expect over the next 7 days? (3) Where is a shortage likely? (4) Is another site able to supply it before an external distributor? (5) Which allocation improves service coverage without increasing waste? (6) What changes after an unexpected event?
• Differentiation versus a general LLM: versioned data, evaluated time-series models, a lot-aware inventory ledger, mathematical constraints, explicit policy goals, approval controls, reproducible plans, and auditable event-driven recomputation. An LLM can optionally explain plans but never approves money movement or edits inventory.
1.1 Problem Statement
Organizations that distribute essential resources frequently operate with incomplete information about future demand and available inventory.
Food banks, community fridges, and emergency relief centers may experience shortages despite sufficient inventory existing elsewhere in their networks.
Conventional inventory systems primarily report current stock. Forecasting tools estimate future demand but often stop before recommending operational decisions.
PantryPulse addresses both problems by predicting potential shortages and recommending feasible actions to reduce them.
The system combines time-series forecasting, inventory projection, uncertainty analysis, and constrained resource allocation into one interactive platform.
1.2 Product Vision
Enable resource-distribution organizations to anticipate shortages and coordinate inventory before essential goods become unavailable.
The product should answer four questions:
• What resources do we currently have, and where?
• How much of each resource will we need over the next seven days?
• Which locations are at risk of running out?
• What feasible actions can reduce those shortages?
1.3 Value Proposition
PantryPulse is not simply a forecasting dashboard.
Its unique value comes from combining:
• Predictive intelligence: A specialized time-series model forecasts demand.
• Uncertainty awareness: Predictions expose possible variation rather than falsely implying certainty.
• Operational optimization: Mathematical solvers identify transfers that respect real constraints.
• Explainable decisions: Users can understand why an action is proposed and what it is expected to improve.
• Continuous replanning: Inventory and demand changes can trigger updated shortage assessments.

1.5 Success Metrics
Technical metrics
• Forecast error compared with a seasonal-naive baseline.
• Prediction-interval coverage, if calibration is implemented.
• Number of inventory or capacity constraints violated by a proposed plan.
• Solver feasibility and optimization status.
• Time needed to regenerate a transfer plan.
Product metrics
• Number of projected shortages identified.
• Forecasted unmet demand before and after optimization.
• Quantity transferred and estimated transportation cost.
• Percentage reduction in forecasted unmet demand within the modeled scenario.
• Time required for a user to understand and approve a recommendation.
These metrics must distinguish simulated projections from observed real-world outcomes.

2.4 P0 priorities revised for research-centrality
MANDATORY P0, BEFORE ADVANCED FORECASTING: (a) One research-grounded allocation formulation incorporating AP and RP goals; (b) normalized multidimensional institution vulnerability attributes with disclosed scoring policy; (c) vulnerability-based weighted deviations; (d) an efficiency-versus-equity objective combining sum of normalized deviations and maximum deviation; (e) a conventional goal-programming baseline using identical inventory, requests, FEFO and capacity data; (f) side-by-side allocation and deprivation metrics; (g) a data-driven demonstration that the method changes at least one decision under a suitably constructed test scenario; and (h) an accurately cited September 2026 publication date.
P0 allocation dataset: for useful comparison groups, seed at least six beneficiary institutions and one or two food-distribution hubs; maintain two food categories, dated lots and 7-day planning assumptions. Use deterministic fictional operational data and explicitly simulated vulnerability indicators; no unsupported claims about real neighborhood poverty.
SUPPORTING P0: inventory/expiry/FEFO, shortage projection, baseline forecast, typed API, objective/constraint tests, interactive comparison view and reset. Chronos-2 is a preferred integration where latency permits; supplier auto-order, FoodRL-style adaptation, external purchasing and actual dispatch cannot displace the EGP-PI centerpiece.
P1: full research fidelity (endogenous attribute-weight model, multiple periods, additional EGP constraints), additional calibration/demand models, donor routing and real procurement approvals.

• Network modes: HUMANITARIAN (default: donated food/essential goods delivered to beneficiary sites) and RETAIL (P1: commercial nodes with alternative business objective). • Personas: Network Coordinator (review shortages/approve a plan); Site Manager (receive donations/adjust stock); Analyst (backtest models and view policy tradeoffs); Procurement Manager (P1 approval of purchases); Demo Administrator (isolated reset).
• P0 scope: 3 sites, 2 products/categories, seeded donated inventory, expiration-dated lots, past demand and incoming donations, 7-day forecasts or labeled fallbacks, first shortage date, FEFO eligibility, internal transfer suggestion, compared supplier reorder proposal, policy-aware optimizer, one simulated disruption and replan, transparent dashboard.
• P1: genuine forecast ensembling with sufficient backtests, lot-level reservations, optional transport routes, authorized small-order automation with safeguards, roles, alerts, additional perishability and multiple day-level shipments. P2: payments, real partner procurement APIs, clinical/nutritional suitability, live food-safety compliance, production tenant isolation and advanced research model reproductions.
• Hard boundary: P0 is decision support and a simulation. Nothing purchases, dispatches, or verifies food safety in the real world.
Priorities:
• P0: Required for the hackathon MVP.
• P1: Valuable enhancement after the core workflow works.
• P2: Future product capability.

3.1 Additional P0 functional requirements for the qualifying discovery
FR-21/P0 — The user can select CLASSICAL_GP versus EGP_PI_INSPIRED for the same immutable scenario, with identical inventory and travel/expiry constraints. AC: both methods generate comparable metrics using the same inputs.
FR-22/P0 — The model loads at least two normalized, disclosed institution-level vulnerability attributes per beneficiary and derives an explicit vulnerability-sensitive priority coefficient. AC: source, scaling and formula are stored; no guessed attributes treated as observed.
FR-23/P0 — The solver measures demand-goal achievement and per-institution normalized underachievement. AC: target = requested eligible quantity; delivered ≤ target; unmet = target − delivered; ratios handle zero demand safely.
FR-24/P0 — The EGP_PI_INSPIRED objective includes an absolute deprivation flag for weighted normalized deficits exceeding a configurable AP threshold and a penalty for exceeding a configured allowed share of flagged institutions. AC: deterministic test crosses threshold and changes AP metrics.
FR-25/P0 — It likewise includes a relative deprivation flag comparing each institution's weighted deficit to the mean of a predefined, nonempty comparison group plus an RP threshold. AC: metrics reflect changes in group reference and allocation; zero/one-member groups are rejected or handled explicitly.
FR-26/P0 — The solver supports a weighted-sum deviation component and a Chebyshev-style worst-deviation component with an explicit coefficient/trade-off and nonzero AP/RP terms. AC: return objective decomposition; objective and solver status are auditable.
FR-27/P0 — Show per-beneficiary demand, delivered units, service ratio, normalized shortfall, vulnerability weight, AP flag, RP flag, and which constraints bound its allocation. AC: explanatory data available through API and accessible UI.
FR-28/P0 — Show the recent-method-versus-classical-GP comparison, not merely optimized-versus-greedy. AC: same snapshot; metrics include total unmet units, max normalized weighted shortfall, AP/RP flagged shares and penalties, projected expiry waste and transportation cost.
FR-29/P0 — The demo and README identify EGP-PI's September 11, 2026 first-publication date, exact paper DOI, which constructs were adapted, and what the implementation does NOT reproduce. AC: judges can observe the newly introduced mechanism changing output.
FR-30/P0 — If the EGP_PI_INSPIRED engine or its AP/RP goal calculations fail, do not label the result as research implementation; display method-unavailable and demonstrate only the baseline with an explicit limitation.

Every correction requires quantity delta, reason, responsible actor (demo actor in MVP), timestamp, previous stock version, new stock version and audit entry. • FR18/P0 — Inventory simulation KPIs: Show before/after projected stockouts, expiration loss, excess stock, service coverage and fulfillment costs for supply delay and demand-spike scenarios. Simulated projections must not be called actual savings.

6. System Architecture
6.V4 Authoritative Extensions
6.5 Research-centred allocation module
Add services/optimization/egp_pi_inspired.py alongside classical_gp.py. Shared constraint builder enforces identical inventory, time window, FEFO, reserve, category and capacity rules for both models; only the goal-programming formulation differs. Shared policy service computes validated input attributes/weights; input snapshot version and model/policy version are immutable.
Pipeline: site requests + lot eligibility → goal/attribute matrix and target denominators → candidate transfer variables → per-site normalized deviations → AP/RP threshold indicator constraints → Chebyshev auxiliary maximum → weighted objective → OR-Tools CP-SAT → per-site explanations + objective decomposition. Keep integer fixed-point scaling explicit and test against small hand-solved examples.
RUNTIME: OR-Tools is the implementation library, not a new technology. EGP-PI's September 2026 AP/RP/attribute-integrating modeling mechanism is the qualifying discovery. If model inference fails, baseline forecasting can still feed this solver.

• Frontend: Next.js App Router + React + TypeScript + Tailwind + shadcn/ui; Recharts for demand/supply charts; optional MapLibre for transfer flows, Motion for state changes and React Flow for process visualization.
• Backend: Python FastAPI + Pydantic; organized as domain modules (inventory ledger and expiry, forecasts, shortage projection, candidate generator, OR-Tools optimizer, policy/approval, scenarios). One deployment, not microservices.
• Database: Postgres on Neon or Supabase; SQLAlchemy/Alembic for schemas/migrations. Vercel hosts Next.js; Railway or another compatible Python worker hosts FastAPI/Chronos. Small local SQLite or precomputed forecast fixtures can support demo fallback.
• Model choices: Chronos-2 when feasible, seasonal-naive baseline, optional P1 weighted ensemble. OR-Tools CP-SAT for resource assignment; add OR-Tools Routing in P1 only if route time windows become important. Gemini optional for typed natural-language scenario creation, with Pydantic validation and no authority to submit purchases.
• Data flow: observed requests + donations + lot ledger + supplier schedules → validated provenance + immutable data snapshot → two forecast targets → dated shortage projection → internal-vs-external candidate scoring → multiobjective solve → DRAFT plan and recommendations → operator approval/simulated events → stale-plan invalidation/replan.
• Repository: apps/web; apps/api/routers; apps/api/services/{forecast,inventory,shortage,optimization,procurement}; apps/api/models; apps/api/schemas; apps/api/migrations; data/demo; tests/unit; tests/integration; tests/e2e; docs/architecture.
6.1 Architectural Style
Use a modular full-stack architecture:
• Next.js handles the user interface.
• FastAPI exposes versioned application endpoints.
• PostgreSQL stores operational records, forecasts, scenarios, and plans.
• A forecasting adapter wraps Chronos-2 or a fallback model.
• An optimization service wraps OR-Tools.
• An inventory projection service performs deterministic calculations.
The services can initially live in one FastAPI codebase. Microservices are unnecessary for the MVP.

6.3 Data Flow
• Demand history and inventory data are imported or seeded.
• The data layer validates and stores observations.
• The forecasting service generates or retrieves demand forecasts.
• The inventory service projects future balances for each site/category.
• The shortage engine identifies possible deficits.
• The optimizer receives transferable inventory, shortages, and constraints.
• OR-Tools produces a feasible allocation or reports failure.
• The resulting plan is stored and returned.
• The frontend displays the recommendation and relevant evidence.
• Scenario changes repeat the necessary downstream calculations.

6.4 Architectural Rules
• The LLM, if added, must never directly modify inventory or approve transfers.
• All quantitative inputs must be validated on the backend.
• Forecast runs must be versioned.
• Optimization must be reproducible from saved inputs.
• Existing forecasts must not be silently modified by new scenarios.
• Approved plans must be revalidated against current inventory.
• User-facing quantities must always have an explicit category and unit.
• Model failure must not prevent users from inspecting existing data.

9. Forecasting Specification
9.V4 Authoritative Extensions
• Forecast independent DAILY series keyed by site/product/target_type. DEMAND means total requested units including unmet requests; DONATION means unpredictable incoming donated units. Confirmed purchased shipments are separately tracked scheduled supplies. Never treat fulfilled-only volume as unconstrained demand, or an uncertain donation as guaranteed stock.
• P0 primary: Chronos-2 on relevant history if install/inference feasible; mandatory seasonal-naive baseline and fallback. Retain chronological rolling-origin backtests and compare MAE, bias, and WAPE where denominator is valid. Missing dates are flagged; no silent zero-filling. Covariates must be known or forecast for the horizon. Sparse history produces LOW_CONFIDENCE rather than fabricated precision.
• P1 adaptive forecasting: combine Chronos, seasonal naive, and optionally gradient boosting with recent-error inverse-MAE weights or a simple expert-weighting policy with minimum sample requirements. Report weights and backtest outcomes. This is FoodRL-inspired but not reinforcement learning.
• Projected usable inventory at date t = eligible stock now + confirmed arrivals up to t + scenario-assumed donations up to t + transfers-in up to t − cumulative forecast requests up to t − transfers-out up to t. Report confirmed and uncertain supply separately. Available stock must not double-count reserved items, ordered-but-not-received goods, or an arrival already included in on-hand.
• Deficit(site,product,t) = max(0, −projected_balance). First shortage day is the earliest date with projected deficit; do not sum overlapping cumulative deficits across multiple days as though independent shortages.
• Uncertainty display: model p10/p50/p90 quantiles or empirically validated time-series prediction intervals, with method name. Summing daily p90 values creates a conservative scenario—not a calibrated 90% joint stockout chance. Do not display numerical failure probabilities absent calibration.
9.1 Prediction Target
The model predicts daily requested resource units for each site/category pair.
Each pair is treated as a time series identified by:
series_id = site_id + category_id
9.2 Input Features
Required:
• Historical daily demand.
• Site identifier.
• Category identifier.
• Calendar date.
Optional:
• Day of week.
• Holiday/event indicators.
• Planned closures.
• Forecast weather.
• Other future-known covariates.
Covariates must be known or forecast for the future horizon. Actual future outcomes must never leak into the forecasting inputs.
9.3 Model Selection
Primary: Chronos-2.
Baseline: Seasonal naive, using values from the previous comparable weekday.
Fallback: Seasonal naive, or a simpler moving-average model when data is insufficient.
Chronos-2's public interface supports long-format DataFrames, quantile levels, and optional future covariates.
The application should isolate model-specific code behind a common forecasting interface so the model can be replaced without changing the API contract.
9.4 Data Preparation
• Enforce one daily record per site/category/date.
• Sort observations chronologically.
• Reject duplicate keys.
• Flag missing dates rather than treating them automatically as zero demand.
• Separate real observations from synthetic and proxy data.
• Clip invalid negative values only after recording a validation error; do not silently repair source data.
• Evaluate performance on future periods not included in the model's historical context.
9.5 Forecast Output
For each day:
• Central demand estimate.
• Optional lower and upper quantiles.
• Model identifier.
• Forecast generation timestamp.
• Data cutoff date.
• Fallback flag.
Resource counts may be integer-valued in the UI, but preserve underlying numerical forecast values for evaluation.
9.6 Uncertainty and Calibration
The MVP may initially use model-generated quantiles.
A P1 enhancement may calibrate intervals using chronological residuals or appropriate time-series conformal techniques.
The system must distinguish:
• Raw model quantiles.
• Empirically calibrated prediction intervals.
• User-specified conservative planning buffers.
Do not claim that an interval represents a calibrated probability until evaluation supports that interpretation.
9.7 Forecast Evaluation
Evaluate using chronological holdout windows.
Metrics:
• Mean absolute error.
• Weighted absolute percentage error, with appropriate handling of zero-demand groups.
• Bias.
• Prediction-interval empirical coverage, when intervals are evaluated.
Compare Chronos-2 against at least one simple baseline.
If Chronos does not outperform the baseline on the selected data, the application should report that result and use the more suitable model.
10. Shortage Detection Specification
10.V4 Authoritative Extensions
• Products have explicit base units; quantities are nonnegative integer multiples of a unit. Do not add water bottles to meal counts without a category/unit transformation.
• Each inventory lot stores site, product, on_hand, reserved, received_at, expires_on (optional), safety_status, and provenance. Eligible(lot,as_of,expected_delivery) = max(0,on_hand−reserved) only if safety_status ELIGIBLE, not expired under the local cutoff and arrival/use window meets restrictions. No product is automatically declared safe solely because its date has not passed.
• Dynamic expiry resembles a TTL for computed availability, NOT database deletion. Expired lots remain in storage, audit and spoilage reports; expiration is evaluated against dates/time zones on every query or by a deterministic materialized view with invalidation.
• FEFO (First Expired, First Out): allocate eligible lots in ascending expiration date, then receipt time, then lot ID. Lots expiring before expected receipt/use are excluded; reservations prevent the same lot serving two plans. Transfer eligibility also respects minimum donor reserve and inbound ETA.
• Inventory events form an append-only ledger. Plans do not move stock. Approved reservations reduce transferable balance, not physical on-hand. Receipt/completion updates physical stock in a transaction. Unique idempotency keys prevent duplicate movements. Outbound and inbound sides of transfers must reconcile.
10.1 Projected Inventory
For a site, category, and target date:
Projected inventory = starting eligible inventory + confirmed incoming supply − projected demand − committed outgoing supply + committed incoming transfers
All terms must refer to the same unit and calendar period.
10.2 Shortage Classification
A projected shortage exists when the computed balance falls below zero.
The backend should expose:
• First projected shortage date.
• Magnitude of projected deficit.
• Forecast method.
• Whether the estimate used central or conservative assumptions.
Suggested UI categories:
• No projected deficit: Central and conservative projections remain nonnegative.
• Potential deficit: Central forecast remains nonnegative but conservative assumptions create a deficit.
• Projected deficit: Central forecast creates a negative projected balance.
• Insufficient data: Reliable projection could not be constructed.
These categories are operational indicators, not independently calibrated probabilities.
10.3 Important Accounting Rules
• Do not double-count a transfer as both scheduled inbound supply and inventory already on hand.
• Exclude expired or already reserved inventory where applicable.
• Approved but incomplete transfers are commitments, not completed physical movements.
• Treat future inbound supplies according to their recorded certainty/status.
• Recalculate projections when relevant inventory or supply information changes.
• Do not add daily quantile bounds together and claim the result is a calibrated aggregate interval.
11. Resource Optimization Specification
11.V4 Authoritative Extensions
11.7 EGP-PI-inspired mathematical subset — P0 implementation contract
Goal indices: beneficiary i, resource product p, optional day t. Let demand target D_i,p>0 be recorded requested units, allocation A_i,p integer with 0≤A_i,p≤D_i,p; deviation U_i,p=D_i,p−A_i,p; normalized deficit δ_i,p=U_i,p/max(1,D_i,p). Handle zero target as excluded from deprivation denominator, without pretending it is deprived.
Vulnerability matrix: normalized attribute a_i,k in [0,1], attribute coefficients c_k≥0 with Σc_k=1. P0 derived weight v_i=1+λΣ_k c_k a_i,k, λ≥0. Define weighted deviation z_i,p=v_i δ_i,p. This fixed, auditable weight formula APPROXIMATES the paper's richer endogenous attribute-based weighting; it is not the full EGP-PI formulation.
Weighted goal-programming term W=Σ_i,p z_i,p. Chebyshev component Z≥z_i,p for every positive-target goal. Their combined criterion is B=βW+(1−β)Z, 0≤β≤1, with positive weights selected for baseline and research variants.
Absolute-poverty adaptation: b_i,p^AP is 1 when z_i,p exceeds configurable τ_AP. Let F_AP=(Σ b^AP)/N and AP_excess=max(0,F_AP−cap_AP). Penalize AP_excess with γ_AP>0.
Relative-poverty adaptation: within documented group g(i,p), compute group mean z̄_g,p. b_i,p^RP=1 when z_i,p−z̄_g,p exceeds τ_RP; F_RP=(Σ b^RP)/N and RP_excess=max(0,F_RP−cap_RP). Penalize RP_excess with γ_RP>0. Group must have enough institutions for meaningful comparison and must not leak hidden protected traits.
Research-inspired objective: minimize B + γ_AP·AP_excess + γ_RP·RP_excess + ε_cost·transfer_cost + ε_waste·projected_waste, with normalized/scaled comparable units and explicitly published coefficients. AP/RP flags enter solver as reified integer threshold constraints, not only post-hoc UI annotations. Constraints for conservation, dated FEFO eligibility, donor reserves, inbound lead times and receiving capacities apply identically to CLASSICAL_GP and EGP_PI_INSPIRED.
CP-SAT implementation: represent normalized ratios and averages using fixed-point integers and cross multiplication; use explicit bounds for big-M or reification, document strict-threshold tie semantics, validate equivalence against hand-computed fixtures. Avoid floating-point constraints in the integer solver.
Comparator CLASSICAL_GP: minimize a predeclared conventional weighted or maximum normalized deviation WITHOUT vulnerability-derived AP/RP goals, but keep the same feasible inventory/transport region. Compare on exactly the same input and report that an EGP-PI-inspired allocation can improve specific deprivation measures while worsening other metrics.
RESEARCH FIDELITY: Paper's complete model includes endogenously determined vulnerability terms and additional interperiod/goal formulations. The above is a runnable limited adaptation of the newly published method, NOT a full reproduction of equations (8)–(20) or (21)–(38); cite the specific paper and maintain this distinction in every publication and demo.

• At stockout risk, find qualified internal donors first: enough available eligible lots after local reserve; same product/unit; transfer can arrive before need and before lot expires; destination has receiving capacity. Compare candidates against external supplier offers by expected arrival, quantity, cost, minimum order, available budget and whether the transfer creates a shortage for its donor.
• Do not assert internal transfer always wins; a supplier may be faster, cheaper or less harmful. The V3 preference is: prioritize internal transfer if feasible, earlier and consistent with policy. If no beneficial internal option, generate an external reorder proposal; do not auto-purchase in P0.
• Core decision variables: integer transfer units x(origin,destination,lot,day), proposed external order quantity y(supplier,site,product,day), unmet units u(site,product,day), plus optional retained/perished inventory. Hard constraints: stock conservation; lot eligibility/FEFO; donor safety reserves; receiving capacity; transfer ETA; vendor lead days/minimum quantities; maximum budget; no self-transfers; no purchase without authorization.
• P0 objective: lexicographic (1) minimize weighted unmet units, (2) minimize worst site shortage fraction/maximize minimum service coverage, (3) minimize expected wasted expiring stock, movement/order costs and unnecessary actions. Weights must be stated, bounded and policy-approved. For humanitarian mode, any vulnerability weights are simulated until supported by data; for retail, use a business service-level policy instead.
• Solver reports OPTIMAL only if proven by OR-Tools; FEASIBLE if valid but not proven; UNKNOWN and INFEASIBLE have informative statuses. Unavoidable shortages appear as slack/unmet demand rather than impossible invented stock.
• For proof of value, benchmark NO_ACTION, NEAREST_SURPLUS, GREEDY_LARGEST_SHORTAGE, and OPTIMIZED under identical inputs, comparing remaining unmet units, minimum site coverage, projected expiry, cost, delivery lateness and constraint violations.
• A replenishment policy may detect that the projected stock will fall below a threshold within the lead time. The system calculates proposed order quantity from deficit and target days of cover, taking into account confirmed arrivals and eligible internal transfers.
• Purchase order lifecycle: DRAFT → REQUIRES_APPROVAL or AUTO_APPROVED_SIMULATED (P0 isolated sandbox) → APPROVED → SUBMITTED (P1 authorized integration only) → CONFIRMED → RECEIVED; may be REJECTED/CANCELLED. Large or unusual orders require explicit human approval.
• P0 'automatic small order' means an approval-decision simulation displayed in the UI, with no real vendor request or payment. P1 auto-submit needs authorized organization opt-in, operator permissions, per-order amount cap, rolling/day spend cap, currency, supplier allowlist, product allowlist, approval/audit policy, and idempotency. Fail closed if any condition is missing.
• Vendor lead time and available price are time-bound assumptions; service should distinguish estimate from confirmation. No proposed/approved but unreceived purchase becomes current inventory automatically.
11.1 Optimization Objective
Minimize a weighted combination of:
• Forecasted unmet demand.
• Transportation or movement costs.
• Unnecessary transfers.
Shortage reduction should take priority over minor transportation savings when that matches the organization's selected policy.
A lexicographic optimization strategy can first minimize weighted unmet demand and then minimize cost among solutions with the best shortage objective.
11.2 Decision Variables
Let:
x(i,j,c) = quantity of resource category c transferred from site i to site j.
Each decision variable is a nonnegative integer.
Optional variables represent remaining shortages after allocation.
11.3 Hard Constraints
The optimizer must enforce:
• No negative inventory.
• No transferring more stock than is eligible and available.
• Donor minimum-reserve requirements.
• Destination receiving capacity.
• Maximum total transfer quantity.
• Category compatibility.
• No self-transfer.
• Any explicitly modeled delivery-time restrictions.
• Optional vehicle capacity constraints.
11.4 Soft Objectives
Subject to those hard constraints, the optimizer attempts to:
• Reduce weighted unmet demand.
• Reduce the number of unnecessary movements.
• Reduce estimated transportation costs.
• Respect policy-defined priorities.
11.5 Solver Outcomes
Possible statuses:
• OPTIMAL: A mathematically optimal solution was proven for the defined model.
• FEASIBLE: A valid solution was found, but optimality was not established.
• INFEASIBLE: No solution satisfies all hard constraints.
• UNKNOWN: Solver did not produce a conclusive result.
For unavoidable shortages, unmet demand should be represented as an explicit slack variable where appropriate, so the solver can return a best-effort allocation rather than declaring an otherwise useful scenario infeasible.
11.6 Baseline Comparison
Include a simple comparison such as:
• No redistribution.
• Equal-share allocation.
• Greedy allocation to the largest shortage.
• Optimized allocation.
Measure:
• Unmet units.
• Weighted unmet demand.
• Transfers required.
• Estimated movement cost.
• Constraint violations.
All baseline methods must use the same input data and feasible-resource assumptions.

17. Risks and Mitigations
17.V4 Authoritative Extensions
17.1 New risks specific to the recent discovery
Method fidelity: a simple weighted penalty is not EGP-PI. Mitigate by implementing both AP and RP group threshold indicator constraints and weighted/Chebyshev trade-off, with explicit 'inspired subset' language.
Data provenance and fairness: vulnerability proxy quality, normalization and group membership can introduce unjustified priorities. Use institutional-level simulated values with labeled policy; do not infer individual demographics or assert equitable real-world impact.
Solver model errors: big-M bounds, scaled divisions, threshold equalities and objective coefficients can silently distort AP/RP logic. Unit test with direct enumeration for small fixtures and report unproven optimality honestly.
Small-sample geometry: only three sites often yields trivial RP groups; use at least six recipient institutions and make comparison groups nontrivial.
Eligibility: old forecasting technology does not qualify if this new research method is absent. Put paper DOI and first-publication proof in docs/README and in the demo methodology screen.

• Fairness priorities need documented governance and data provenance; simulated weights do not prove real equity gains. No discrimination inferred from protected attributes. Food expiration does not guarantee edibility or handling compliance. No unverified claim that any physical item is safe or delivery possible.
• Performance goal: solver complete within ~5 seconds for tiny seeded instance; dashboard cached response interactive; hosted model inference can run asynchronously; show fallback if dependencies fail.

19. Design Principles
19.V4 Authoritative Extensions
19.1 Research-derived implementation integrity
Name the method EGP_PI_INSPIRED unless the published constraints are precisely reproduced and validated; do not claim the paper's fairness/efficiency gains on synthetic data. Always identify first-publication date (September 11, 2026), DOI, methods implemented (AP, RP, normalized vulnerability attributes, deviation objective, FEFO) and limitations (fixed proxy weights, limited horizon, synthetic scenario, not EGP-PI full formulation). The recent 2026 discovery must alter solver decisions, not just appear in documentation.

• No external spending or autonomous dispatch by default. Never store supplier/payment tokens in browser. Only explicit production roles may perform P1 approval; audit actors and idempotency. Demo reset only for a demo organization, and demo URLs should not imply real donations accepted.
• Forecast accuracy must be evaluated chronologically; synthetic data must not be presented as measured food-bank demand. Requested demand and fulfilled demand are different signals. Donation forecasts may be especially unstable during disruptions. Intervals/percentages need method and validation.
• Fairness priorities need documented governance and data provenance; simulated weights do not prove real equity gains. No discrimination inferred from protected attributes. Food expiration does not guarantee edibility or handling compliance. No unverified claim that any physical item is safe or delivery possible.
• Model limitations must be visible. Forecast uncertainty is part of the product, not an implementation detail to hide.
• Optimization constraints must be testable. A solver result is only as trustworthy as the input data and encoded rules.
• No fictitious impact claims. Demonstration outcomes based on synthetic or proxy data must be labeled accordingly.
• Human operators retain control. The application proposes plans rather than executing real deliveries automatically.
• One reliable capability is better than several incomplete features. The MVP prioritizes prediction-to-action over unnecessary integrations.
• Every recommendation must be reproducible. Preserve the model, data snapshot, scenario inputs, and solver results.
• Build for graceful degradation. The shortage and optimization modules remain usable when the primary forecasting model is unavailable.
